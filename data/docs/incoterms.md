# Incoterms Guide

Mock policy for the Portside demo. Not real carrier or customs rules.

## Supported terms

Portside supports two delivery terms: DDP and DDU. The merchant chooses one when the label is created. It cannot be changed after the carrier collects the parcel.

## DDP: Delivered Duty Paid

The seller pays import duties and taxes. Portside pays them at clearance and invoices the merchant, adding a disbursement fee of 2% of the duties and taxes, with a minimum of USD 3. The customer pays nothing on delivery, which reduces refused parcels.

## DDU: Delivered Duty Unpaid

The buyer pays import duties and taxes on delivery, usually with a carrier handling fee. The merchant should tell the customer this at checkout. A customer who refuses to pay causes a return to origin, and the merchant pays return shipping.

## Choosing between DDP and DDU

DDP is recommended for the EU, UK and US, where customers commonly expect no charges on delivery. DDU may suit low-value orders to destinations with high de minimis thresholds, such as Australia and New Zealand, where duties are unlikely.

## Who pays when a shipment is held

Under either term, the merchant pays storage fees caused by missing or incorrect documents. Under DDP, the merchant also pays any reassessed duties. Under DDU, reassessed duties are passed on to the buyer.
